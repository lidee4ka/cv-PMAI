TourGenius

Telegram-first Travel Tech MVP

Product & Delivery Lead + UX/UI 11 months Oct 2024 — Aug 2025

Telegram-first Travel Tech MVP: find and book local outdoor events in the Altai Republic

  • Discovery. 25+ interviews. The “map + budget + timing in one place” hypothesis did not hold: people were fine with separate maps and calendars; what stuck was emotion and the unplanned. We rebuilt around emotional matching
  • Scope. Designed emotional matching to recommend events from the catalog — cut it from the launch so we could ship in season
  • Delivery + UX/UI. Backlog with two remote teams; Mini App architecture: search, filters, catalog of events and locations, booking. After shipping features we read impact and decided what stayed in scope
  • Outcome. 256 events and 100 locations, functional MVP. Closed in 2025: the B2C product relied on Telegram as the only channel, while market risk of a full messenger block in Russia rose

Hypothesis · prototype · BEANABOX

Buyer UX optimization for the Mini App catalog

Cursor · HTML/CSS/JS · GitHub Pages BEANABOX · Sep 2026 · 8 iterations in 2 days
Problem

In the app catalog, a buyer struggled to find green coffee for their needs on their own and often had to ask a manager to place the order. Goal: raise self-serve orders from 10–20% to 30–40%

Solution

Framed JTBD and a hypothesis for a new catalog structure, then in ~15 active hours built a clickable prototype in Cursor — 8 iterations: shelves on the left, filter chips, sticky tools, denser lot cards with cart. Goal: validate UX myself without waiting on external engineering

Outcome

Status: prototype ready for testing with 5–7 buyers — self-serve order lift not measured yet. Success criterion: 5 of 7 complete a typical order without a manager. Engineering will get an aligned scenario and spec — not a raw hypothesis

How I work

Discovery → prototype → build → release → measure in prod → roll back or next step. Engineering gets an aligned scenario, not a raw idea

01

Discovery and priorities

I frame the pain, the hypothesis, and the v1 boundary. Priorities via RICE and hard scope: what gives a signal faster and cheaper, and what we deliberately defer

02

UX and a prototype before code

Figma and Figma Jam. Contested flows — a clickable prototype in Cursor to cut risk before engineering. Gemini for copy and draft specs, DeepSeek to stress-test the scenario and edge cases. Stakeholders click through → we cut scope → what remains is a scenario ready for build

03

Specs, tests, release readiness

I write the spec, acceptance criteria, and test cases, and drive delivery to ready. Before ship I lock how we validate in prod: success metric, fail threshold, observation window. In parallel — a rollback plan: what we turn off or revert if impact is negative or the funnel breaks

04

Ship and measure impact in prod

After release I do not stop at “shipped” — I check whether the metric moved in the observation window. If the hypothesis holds — scale it or take the next priority. If not — roll back or shrink scope, do not pile features on an unproven change
